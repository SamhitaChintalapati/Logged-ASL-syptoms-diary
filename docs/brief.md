PRODUCT BRIEF 
App name: Logged: an ALS symptom diary. A simple browser app for logging day-to-day changes. It never diagnoses or interprets symptoms.

The problem (2-3 sentences): People living with ALS (amyotrophic lateral sclerosis) usually go to the doctor every few months. Changes in their symptoms in between visits are easy to forget or hard to describe on the day. And keeping paper notes or phone notes organized and clear is difficult. 

Who it is for : Adults with ALS who still use their phones or laptops, and need a clear record to bring to their visits every few months. Furthermore, a caregiver could also log the symptoms or data for them. This is not for people who want a diagnosis or want their symptoms interpreted. 

Main features (3-6, must-have only):

 Quick log entry: pick a date, tap one category (speech, swallowing, breathing, hands and arms, legs and walking, tiredness, other), rate how hard it was today from 1 to 5, and add an optional short note.
Diary list: entries newest to oldest, with a filter that shows you the categories.
The app should allow you to edit or delete any entries.
Summary for visits: A printable page that shows you the last 90 days, grouped by categories, to show to doctors.
Easy to use design: The app should be easy to navigate and use with large text and buttons.

Not included (out of scope):
Diagnosis, explaining what a symptom means, or predicting how the disease will progress.
Alerts or warnings based on ratings.
Medication reminders.
Any real patient data.
Alerts or warnings based on the symptoms logged.

Data the app will store (all fake/demo data):
Everything is saved only in the browser's local storage on one device. All of it is fake demo data for a made-up user, "Sam, 58", with about 20 sample entries. The app does not store a name, date of birth, contact details, location or diagnosis.
Health, privacy or safety risks and how I will handle them:
The biggest risk is someone relying on the diary when they actually need urgent care, so the app says very clearly what it does not do. For example, if the user reads the ratings as a medical measure or a sign of decline, the app will label ratings "your own rating, not a clinical score". No automatic interpretation, no red "danger" colours, no trend predictions..
How I will know it works (success measure):
The main measure: 3 test users each log an entry in under 30 seconds and print a visit summary without any help.
[ ] Logging one entry takes 5 taps or clicks or fewer
[ ] Entries are still there after closing and reopening the browser
[ ] The 90-day summary for the demo data prints on one page
[ ] Every feature works with the keyboard alone
[ ] The Lighthouse accessibility score is 90 or higher
[ ] The safety banner shows on every page
